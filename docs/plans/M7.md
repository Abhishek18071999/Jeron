# M7: Exit engine and analytics

Status: in progress. M6 part 1 is PR #12; M6 part 2 (fundamentals, combined brains)
waits on inputs from Abhishek and comes after M7.

## Goal (spec section 11)
Pre-open checks, trailing and time stops, broker tradebook import,
backtest-vs-paper-vs-real dashboard, weekly summary.

## Starting point
- The backtest engine and the paper accounts already apply every exit rule of spec
  section 5 (stop, breakeven at +1R, 50% at T1, 2 x ATR(14) closing-basis trail, time
  stop). Real positions in the journal have none of this yet: the journal knows fills,
  P&L and R, but not today's stop or what to do tomorrow morning.
- Jobs are run by hand (`daily`). Spec section 10 asks for a scheduler: EOD scan after
  18:30 IST, pre-open checks at 08:30 IST, weekly revalidation.
- No strategy is live-eligible yet, so the comparison dashboard will mostly show
  backtest vs paper until real trades exist. It still has to work for that day.

## Scope, in build order
1. **Exit plan for real positions** (`app/exits/`, pure). For each open journal
   position, from its fills, its stop (mine, else the signal's), its tier and the
   adjusted bars since entry, the same rules as the engine:
   - stop to breakeven once a close is +1R;
   - T1 at +2R from my average entry (as the engine and the paper accounts measure it
     from their fill): book half;
   - after T1, trail the rest at 2 x ATR(14) below the highest close, closing basis;
   - time stop: swing exits if not +1R within 15 sessions; positional at the
     60-session review (the engine's rule).
   The result per position: today's stop, T1 still open or done, the trail, sessions
   held, and the action for tomorrow's open (hold, sell all, sell half, review), each
   with the reason in plain words. Rule constants come from the engine so they cannot
   drift; a test replays one trade through both and compares.
2. **Pre-open check** (`python -m app.cli preopen`, spec section 5). For every open
   journal position (and, in a short section, every open paper position): the exit
   plan above, plus
   - the stop was hit (the signal's invalidation after entry);
   - results date within 3 sessions (from M6's board meetings);
   - corporate action with an ex-date today or in the next 3 sessions (NSE corporate
     actions already loaded), since it moves price and stop;
   - stale data: if the latest bars are missing, it says so and gives no actions.
   One Telegram message (email backup), stored once per day like other alerts. Nothing
   is sent when there are no open positions, except a one-line "nothing open".
3. **Zerodha tradebook import** (spec section 8). `POST /journal/import` and
   `python -m app.cli tradebook FILE`, plus an upload box on the journal page.
   - Columns as Kite Console exports them: symbol, isin, trade_date, exchange, segment,
     series, trade_type, auction, quantity, price, trade_id, order_id,
     order_execution_time. Equity cash only; F&O and other segments are skipped and
     counted.
   - Each trade becomes a fill, with the broker's trade id stored so a re-import
     never adds it twice. Partial executions of one order are kept as separate fills.
   - Matching: a buy goes to the open journal entry for that symbol; else to the
     latest signal for that symbol whose entry window covers the date (marked taken);
     else a new manual entry. A sell goes to the open entry for that symbol; a sell
     with nothing open is reported, not guessed.
   - Charges per fill are estimated with the backtester's Indian cost model (Zerodha
     delivery rates), labelled as estimated, editable.
   - The import returns what it did: added, already there, skipped, problems.
4. **Backtest vs paper vs real** (spec section 7). Page `/compare` and
   `GET /analytics/compare`. Per strategy version, side by side: stage (backtest,
   paper, small real, full), trades, win rate, expectancy (R), profit factor, average
   hold, max drawdown, and for paper and real the gate status (3 months and 30
   trades; expectancy > 0 and inside the backtest's range). Backtest numbers are the
   run's out-of-sample, after-cost ones. The "expected range" is the 5th to 95th
   percentile of the mean R of N trades resampled from the backtest's out-of-sample
   trades, N = the paper or real trade count.
   When real differs from paper, likely causes are measured, not guessed:
   - slippage: my average entry vs the paper fill for the same signal, in R;
   - skipped signals: R of the paper trades I skipped;
   - late entries: days between the paper entry and my first buy;
   - early or late exits: my exit vs the paper exit for the same signal, in R.
5. **Weekly summary** (spec section 8). After the last session of each week, one
   Telegram message and a section on the journal page:
   - my real edge: closed trades, win rate, average R and P&L this week and to date;
   - mistakes found from the data: stop moved down (my stop below the signal's),
     early exit (sold before stop, T1 or trail said so), skipped winners (skipped
     signals whose paper trade won), late entries;
   - what strict rule-following would have made: the paper results of the same
     signals, next to mine.
   - Stored once per week, like other alerts.
6. **Weekly revalidation** (spec section 6). Weekly: each strategy with paper or real
   trades is checked for rolling 50-trade expectancy < 0 or a drawdown above 1.5 x its
   backtest max. A breach retires the strategy's paper account (no new signals) and
   says so in the weekly summary. Retiring is stored, never deleted.
7. **Scheduler.** A `scheduler` service in Docker Compose (APScheduler, one process):
   `daily` at 19:00 IST, `preopen` at 08:30 IST, weekly summary and revalidation on
   the last trading day of the week after `daily`. All skip NSE holidays and are safe
   to re-run. `docker compose up -d` is then enough; nothing to run by hand.

## Schema changes
- `journal_fills`: `broker_trade_id` (unique, nullable), `source` ("manual" /
  "tradebook"), `charges_estimated`.
- `paper_accounts.status` gains "retired"; `retired_reason` column.
- Alerts reuse `alerts` with keys `preopen:<date>`, `weekly:<week>`.

## Spec readings (please confirm)
- Stages: "small real" when a strategy has real trades and my capital setting is below
  the intended one; "full" only after the small-real gate passes. Jeron shows the stage,
  it never moves money.
- "Review at 60" for positional is treated as the engine treats it: exit at the next
  open if +1R hasn't been reached. Say if you'd rather it only warned.
- Paper positions are in the pre-open message only as a short count by action, since
  they need nothing from me.

## Tests
- Exit plan: breakeven, half at T1, trail ratchets up only, closing basis, time stop
  at 15, review at 60; matches the engine on a replayed trade.
- Pre-open: each check on hand-built positions; stale data gives no actions; stored
  once per day.
- Tradebook: a sample Kite CSV (synthetic, no real account data); re-import adds
  nothing; matching to open entries and to signals; F&O skipped; oversell reported.
- Compare: expected range from resampled trades; gates; divergence causes.
- Weekly: mistakes detected on hand-built journals; stored once per week.
- Revalidation: retire on rolling expectancy and on drawdown.
- Scheduler: jobs registered at the right IST times; holidays skipped.

## Acceptance checks
- On the laptop: import a real Kite tradebook; the journal shows the fills on the right
  entries; re-import adds nothing.
- A pre-open Telegram message arrives at 08:30 IST with today's stop and action per open
  position.
- `/compare` shows backtest vs paper (and real once trades exist) per strategy.
- A weekly summary arrives after Friday's close.
